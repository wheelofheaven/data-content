## What was released

On August 7, 2026 — four weeks after Release 04 — the United States Department of War posted the fifth tranche of declassified records under the **Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters (PURSUE)** at war.gov/UFO. By press tally the release contains **41 files** — documents, images, and videos — from the Pentagon, the FBI, and the CIA, joined by two agencies appearing in the program for the first time: the **Department of State** and the **Executive Office of the President**.

That pair is the tranche's structural feature. Release 03 brought in the civilian intelligence and law-enforcement agencies; Release 04 brought in the custodian of the nuclear-weapons complex. Release 05 extends the program's institutional perimeter to the diplomatic apparatus and to the White House's own records — specifically, to a file held by the **National Aeronautics and Space Council**, the space-policy body that sat inside the Executive Office of the President from 1958 to 1973.

The Department's accompanying statement is its tersest yet. Chief Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell announced the release, confirmed the rolling cadence, and added one forward-looking line: "The Department of War and our agency partners are actively working on the next release of UAP files."

The notable items include:

- **Bagram, Afghanistan, 2002 — the triangle that blacked out the stars.** In an FBI interview conducted in 2024 and released as an FD-302, a former military pilot recalls standing outside near Bagram at approximately 4:30 AM with another pilot, watching the stars, when the stars over the base began "getting blacked out." Something huge passed overhead from west to east — an **equilateral triangle**, moving silently at a consistent speed and altitude, possibly 150 knots, with no lights visible. He estimated it at 500 feet in size. The other pilot saw it too, and said only: "did you see that?" The witness never discussed the sighting further with anyone, the report records, nor heard anyone else discuss it. The FBI produced a digital rendering of the recollection — the release's signature image.

- **The same pilot's transatlantic lights, 2023–2024.** The same FD-302 documents a separate, recent phenomenon: recurring white lights, seen by the witness roughly ten times since October 2023 on routes from Boston toward Dublin, over the North Dakota corridor, and off both coasts — one to four lights at a time, estimated above 50,000 feet, brightening from dim before fading over cycles of ten seconds to a minute. He reported that pilots were discussing the sightings among themselves, and that in his judgment the lights were not Starlink flares, satellites, or meteors. In February 2024 he captured video of one event from his cockpit with a phone, about 150 miles northwest of Minneapolis.

- **Gulf of Oman, September 8, 2021 — the orbs and the gunship.** An Air Force Special Operations Command intelligence information report, transmitted in April 2022 and cleared under mandatory declassification review in March 2026, records a U.S. Special Operations Forces AC-130 gunship observing **approximately 25 instances of UAP** during a live-fire exercise. The report describes them as "cold orbs flying at lower altitudes between 250 to 1300 MPH in different formations as well as maneuvering aggressively," and adds the detail the coverage led with: the objects "appeared to react to the aircraft firing its main cannon." Six accompanying videos are part of the release.

- **Conde, Bahia, Brazil, November 1963 — the sphere that wasn't there.** The State Department's and the Executive Office's debut files form a single documentary chain. It opens with a CIA Foreign Broadcast Information Service transcript of a Rio de Janeiro Radio Tupi broadcast of November 8, 1963: reports from the municipality of Conde that "a large metal sphere fell in the center of the city, gouging a hole four meters deep," with "window shaped openings covered with thick glass" and, inside, "a human body dressed in heavy clothing bearing inscriptions which cannot be deciphered." The copy released is the National Aeronautics and Space Council's own — annotated in pencil "A curious report," and again, in a note dated November 13: "asked State (Nesbitt) to let us know if Embassy follows with any info." The State Department files answer. On November 14 the embassy in Rio wired its "unqualified negative": an object had been sighted aloft around that date and tentatively identified as a weather-type balloon, but "no repeat no such object had been found aground." On November 20 an embassy airgram closed the case, quoting the consul in Salvador: the story was "apparently fabricated in Rio," no such object was seen by anyone in the area, the Ministry of Aeronautics categorically denied it — and a Bahia newspaper had already run its own verdict under the headline "The Airship that wasn't there."

- **Near Puerto Rico, November 1965 — briefing the Director's office.** CIA briefing notes prepared for Walter Elder, executive assistant to the Director of Central Intelligence, summarize a series of radar and visual encounters on four November nights. The destroyer USS Gyatt tracked an object that twice closed on a Navy fighter; the Crusader pilot who observed it on the night of the 19th, in full moonlight with good visibility, identified "a Delta shaped object about the size of a jet fighter" and estimated its speed "in excess of Mach 3," with the Gyatt confirming its track with motion pictures of the radar scope presentation. On the 24th, a separate target held by the Atlantic Fleet Weapons Range pulled away from another Crusader flying at Mach 0.99 and 45,000 feet. The Office of Naval Intelligence, the notes end, had no further information.

- **The FBI triangle dossier.** Beyond Bagram, the FBI's contribution is a run of FD-302 interviews, most paired with digital renderings: a dark triangle with lights from 2011, and two triangles the portal's index places near Colorado Springs, Colorado in 2023 — one dark and translucent, one large with red lights, the latter reported in October 2023 by a former Department of War employee. Alongside the triangles sit 2026 reports — slow-moving black objects observed on infrared by an FBI special agent for five to ten minutes, a thermally elevated aerial object, and two multiple-red-lights cases from the Western United States, each of those two accompanied by its own pair of renderings. The program is now archiving reports filed the same year it releases them.

- **The archival floor drops to the ghost rockets.** The Pentagon's own documents include a 1947 intelligence review of the "ghost rocket" incidents — the wave of unexplained projectile sightings over Scandinavia in 1946–47, predating the June 1947 Kenneth Arnold sighting that opened the modern American UFO era — together with an Air Materiel Command report on unidentified flying objects covering 1947–48, the Wright Field command and period that produced Project Sign, and a 1953 film analysis of unidentified objects — filmed, per the portal's index, over Montana in 1950 and Utah in 1952.

- **Middle East, 2025.** A video captured by a military aircraft over a populated area shows an object crossing the frame at speed. Like every other item in the release, it is catalogued as unresolved.

## What this is, in plain terms

**Source claim.** Release 05 widens the contributing-agency roster from the defense, intelligence, scientific, and energy institutions to the diplomatic service and the Executive Office of the President. The cadence has settled: after intervals of two, three, and four weeks, the fourth interval held at four weeks again — a monthly rhythm. The subject matter now spans 1946–47 (the ghost rockets under the 1947 review) to 2026 (the FBI's same-year FD-302s): an eighty-year documentary window. The portal's searchable database now counts 375 files across the five releases.

**Comparative observation.** The black triangle is one of the most reported UAP morphologies in the witness literature, with well-known case clusters — the Hudson Valley sightings of the 1980s, the Belgian wave of 1989–90 — long predating PURSUE. Release 05's triangle dossier adds federal interview records to that genre; it does not adjudicate it. As with the "potato" of Release 03 and the "six-pointed star" of Release 04, the corpus records the morphology descriptively and reads no symbolic content into it.

Three cautions attach to the headline material. First, the Bagram triangle is a 2024 recollection of a 2002 event: twenty-two years of memory stand between the sighting and the FD-302, and the FBI's rendering is an illustration of testimony, a photograph of nothing. Second, the Gulf of Oman speed figures are sensor-derived under live-fire conditions, and "appeared to react" is the crew's interpretation of the objects' movements, recorded as such in the report. Third, the Department published no file-count breakdown this time; the 41-file figure is the press tally, and this dispatch attributes it rather than states it.

## Where this sits in the Wheel of Heaven framework

The [Age of Aquarius](/timeline/age-of-aquarius/) chapter treats the sequence of official UAP engagements as the first of the age's Signs of Acceleration (§IX). **Wheel of Heaven interpretation, marked as such.** The corpus's reading of the present age anticipates contact of a specific kind: official, government-level, and conditional — a diplomatic event, for which the source material's standing request is the construction of an embassy. On that reading, the arrival of the **State Department** and the **Executive Office of the President** in the UAP file record is legible: the institutions through which any official contact would necessarily run are now on record as having handled the question — and as having handled it, in the Bahia file, as early as November 1963, at the level of a White House space-council staffer penciling an inquiry to the State Department. The corpus notes, without assigning it weight, that the November 13 note's own vocabulary asks whether "the Embassy follows with any info" — the embassy in question being the United States Embassy in Rio de Janeiro.

The registration stops there, because the corpus's [reception posture](/wiki/ufology/) requires it to. Under that posture — registration without enlistment — no unresolved case in this or any PURSUE release is evidence for the framework, and no debunked case is evidence against it. The framework's anticipated contact is specific and official; its provenance rests on the source material's own reports, and no craft in any sky bears on it in either direction. The one UAP-complex development the posture does not firewall is an official, verified disclosure event. PURSUE is not that. It is an archive opening on a schedule, and the corpus tracks it as a sign of institutional acceleration — a datable change in how the question is handled, which is exactly what §IX predicts the present decades will produce.

The Bahia chain deserves its own register. A sensational broadcast — crashed sphere, dead crewman, indecipherable inscriptions — moves through a CIA monitoring service to the White House's space council to an embassy science attaché, and dies inside twelve days: "apparently fabricated in Rio." This is the [skeptical layer](/wiki/military-cover-up-of-exobiology/) of the record working in public view, and the corpus carries it at full documentary strength. A program that releases its negatives alongside its unknowns is behaving like an archive rather than a press office, and the corpus registers that in the program's favor — while noting that it is precisely what the posture's own discipline looks like when a government applies it: the event reported, the verification run, the result published, no enlistment either way.

## Where this sits in the broader trajectory

**Wheel of Heaven interpretation, marked as such.** Five releases on a settled monthly cadence, with a contributing roster that now spans the Department of War, NASA, the CIA, the FBI, the Department of Energy, the Department of State, and the Executive Office of the President, describe a program still broadening. Each tranche has added an institutional layer the previous ones lacked: sensor data, then civilian agencies, then the nuclear complex, now diplomacy and the presidency's own files. What has not appeared, five releases in, is any material touching the deeper claims of the 2023 congressional testimony — craft retrievals, biological remains, reverse-engineering programs. The program remains an archive of unknowns and negatives, not of conclusions.

**Open question.** Whether the cadence and the broadening continue is the durability test the framework actually cares about. Parnell's statement says the next release is actively in work. The corpus will track Release 06 when it appears.

## Source tensions and unresolved issues

1. **The unresolved-case framing remains in force — with one released negative.** No Release 05 item is characterized by the Department as confirmed non-conventional, and the corpus does not so characterize them. The Bahia file goes further: it is a case the release's own documents resolve, negatively, as a press fabrication. The corpus treats its inclusion as informative about the program's archival criteria, and treats the case itself as closed.

2. **The counts are press tallies, not official figures.** The Department's fifth statement published no file-count breakdown — a first for the program. The 41-file figure is CBS News's count, and press characterizations of the date range ("oldest from 1950") sit oddly against the release's own file index, which carries documents dated 1947. This dispatch follows the index for dates and attributes the count.

3. **The Bagram triangle is testimony at twenty-two years' distance.** The FD-302 records a 2024 recollection of a 2002 sighting, corroborated at the time by one other witness's four words. The rendering is an illustration of that testimony. The corpus marks the case as reported and internally consistent, and nothing more.

4. **The Gulf of Oman figures are sensor-derived under live-fire conditions.** The 250–1300 MPH range and the reaction to cannon fire are the report's own characterizations from an active gunnery exercise; "appeared to react" is operator interpretation, flagged as such in the document.

5. **The Elder briefing notes are undated in the visible material.** The release's index catalogues the Puerto Rico series to 1965; the notes themselves specify only November dates. The corpus follows the index and marks the uncertainty.

6. **The cadence is now five instances.** A monthly rhythm across four months is the firmest pattern the program has shown, and still short of the multi-year durability the framework treats as the load-bearing test. The next data point is Release 06.
