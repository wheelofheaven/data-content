Isaiah is the longest of the prophetic books and one of the most quoted in the New Testament. This translation covers every chapter of the book that the Raëlian canon cites:

- **Isaiah 6**, the prophet's vision of YHWH enthroned in the temple, attended by seraphim who call "Holy, holy, holy". The notes compare it with the throne-vision of Ezekiel 1.
- **Isaiah 24–27**, the "Little Apocalypse": the judgment of "the host of the height on high", death swallowed up for ever (25:8), "your dead shall live" (26:19), and YHWH's defeat of Leviathan, "the fleeing serpent, the twisting serpent" (27:1), whose epithets match those of Lôtanu in the Ugaritic Baal Cycle.
- **Isaiah 13–15**, the oracles against Babylon and Moab: the host gathered "from the end of the skies" (13:5) and the taunt over the fallen "day star, son of the dawn" (14:12).
- **Isaiah 29 and 32**: woe to Ariel and the sealed scroll; the king who reigns in righteousness and the spirit poured out from on high.
- **Isaiah 40–45 and 48**: "Comfort my people", the one who measured the waters and the skies, the first Servant Song, "you are my witnesses", the idol-maker's block of wood, Cyrus the anointed, and the one who forms light and creates darkness.
- **Isaiah 53**, the suffering servant.
- **Isaiah 54 and 65**: the covenant of peace, and the new skies and the new earth.

The English follows the Masoretic text. Later readings, such as the identification of the heavenly host with fallen angels or the Christian reading of the servant, are set out in the notes. Verses with a note end in a bracketed marker `[ ]`; select it to open the note.

See also the [reference corpus of the Raëlian canon](/wiki/reference-corpus-of-the-raelian-canon/) and the [Genesis translation](/library/genesis-woh/).
