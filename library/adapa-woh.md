*Adapa and the South Wind* is a Babylonian myth about how humans lost the chance of eternal life. Adapa, a sage of Eridu who serves the god Ea, breaks the wing of the South Wind with a spoken curse and is summoned to heaven to answer to Anu. Ea warns him to refuse the food and water Anu will offer, calling them food and water of death. They are in fact food and water of life; Adapa refuses them, and humanity remains mortal.

The story survives in four fragments written over roughly a thousand years: Fragment A from the Amarna archive in Egypt (EA 356, 14th century BCE), Fragment B from Nineveh, and Fragments C and D from later Babylonian copies. Gaps in the tablets are marked in the text. The notes compare the story with Genesis 3:22, where YHWH-Elohim keeps the first humans from the tree of life. Both stories explain why humans do not live forever, but they place the decision on opposite sides of the line between gods and humans.
