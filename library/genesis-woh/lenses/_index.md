## The four lenses

1. **Hebrew.** The Masoretic text with a transliteration under each word, so you can see what the verse actually says before anyone translates it.
2. **Literal.** The Wheel of Heaven literal translation of the [Hebrew](/library/genesis-woh/). It keeps what the standard versions smooth away, such as the plural *Elohim*. Every chapter has been reviewed and signed off.
3. **Reading.** The passage retold in plain sentences as the Messages understand it. It follows the meaning, not the words, and it quotes *The Book Which Tells the Truth* wherever the book speaks.
4. **Meaning.** A short explanation of each passage in modern language, for a reader who has never opened Genesis.

Each chapter opens on the literal translation. Move the slider to change every verse at once, or step a single verse on its own. Your choice is remembered on this device.

## Canon first

The Reading and the Meaning follow the dictated Messages wherever they comment on a passage, and they cite the paragraph they rely on. Where the Messages are silent, the page says so plainly before it offers a Wheel of Heaven reading. Raël's later commentary is cited as his opinion, never as the canon.

## Claim labels

Every sentence in the Reading and the Meaning carries one of four labels, shown as a coloured dot you can tap; pointing at a sentence underlines it in the same colour:

- **Direct:** stated in the text itself and uncontroversial.
- **Framework:** a premise of the Messages that mainstream scholarship does not share.
- **Inferred:** a reasonable reading of the text that it does not state outright.
- **Speculative:** a Wheel of Heaven synthesis that goes beyond any single source.

The Reading and Meaning lenses are being written for the primeval history (chapters 1–11) first. Chapters 1–5, from the six days of creation through Cain and Abel to the generations from Adam to Noah, are published in all four lenses. Each further chapter is published after review and the founder's sign-off; until then it shows the Hebrew and the literal translation.
