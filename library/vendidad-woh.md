The **Vendidad** (*Vīdēvdād*, "the law against the demons") is the only book of the Sasanian Avesta canon that survives complete. Most of its twenty-two chapters, called *fargards*, are priestly law on purity and its breach. The second fargard is different: it is a narrative about Yima, the first king, and the earliest Iranian account of a refuge built against the destruction of the world.

In this fargard Ahura Mazda offers Yima, son of Vivanghat, the Religion; Yima declines to carry it, and receives rule over the world instead. Under his rule there is neither cold nor heat, disease nor death, and three times the earth grows too crowded and Yima enlarges it. Then Ahura Mazda warns him of the evil winters to come, with frost, deep snow and the flood of meltwater after them, and instructs him to build the **Vara**, an enclosure "the length of a race-course on each of its four sides", and to bring into it the seeds of people, animals, plants and fires, the greatest, best and fairest of each, keeping out every mark of Angra Mainyu. The fargard ends with the Vara's self-created lights and the life of those within it.

This is a translation of Fargard 2 only. The English follows Geldner's Avestan text and numbering. Square brackets mark a repeated formula that the source abbreviates and the translation restores, angle brackets mark a word the editors supply, and "(?)" marks a word whose meaning is uncertain. The notes compare the Vara with Noah's ark and the boat of the Mesopotamian flood stories, a parallel scholars have discussed since Darmesteter and whose origin remains disputed. Verses with a note end in a bracketed marker `[ ]`; select it to open the note.
