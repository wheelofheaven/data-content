**The Sumerian King List (Wheel of Heaven Translation)** is rendered from
the ETCSL composite transliteration (ETCSL 2.1.1) with the source and the
English translation displayed in parallel.

The composition opens with the formula that frames everything after it:
**"After the kingship descended from heaven"**. Kingship is instituted at
**Eridug** and passes through the antediluvian cities — Bad-tibira,
Larag, Zimbir, Curuppag — whose kings reign for impossibly long spans of
thousands of years, until **the flood swept over**. Kingship then
descends from heaven a second time, and the list resumes with the
post-diluvian dynasties — Kish, Uruk (including **Gilgamesh**), Ur, and
onward down to Isin — with reign-lengths contracting toward historical
scale as the list approaches its own present. For the Wheel of Heaven
frame the List is the Mesopotamian scaffold of the antediluvian-longevity
and flood-reset pattern that the corpus reads alongside Genesis 5–11: an
institutional memory, in king-list form, of the epochs the biblical
genealogies compress.

**Lines with commentary carry a bracketed marker `[ ]` at the end of the
translated line. Click the marker to open the commentary panel.** Per-line
commentary documents lexical decisions, the manuscript variants of the
composite text, the reign-number traditions, and the labeled comparative
links to the Hebrew Bible and the wider Mesopotamian cluster.

The single composition (435 lines) is signed off as **complete** as of
2026-07-31.
