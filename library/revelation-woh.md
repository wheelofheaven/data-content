The Book of Revelation is the last book of the New Testament, a series of visions given to John on the island of Patmos. The Raëlian canon reads it verse by verse in *Let's Welcome the Extra-Terrestrials*. This translation covers every chapter the canon cites:

- **Revelation 1**: John's vision of one like a son of man among seven lampstands, whose voice is "like the sound of many waters", and who comes "with the clouds" (1:7).
- **Revelation 4**: a door opened in heaven, the throne, the twenty-four elders and the four living creatures full of eyes (lion, ox, man, eagle), crying "Holy, holy, holy".
- **Revelation 5**: the book in the right hand of the one seated on the throne, "sealed with seven seals", and the Lamb standing "as though it had been slain", who is worthy to open it.
- **Revelation 6**: the opening of the seals: the four horsemen, and at the sixth seal the sun turned black, the moon to blood and the stars falling to earth.
- **Revelation 7**: the sealing of the servants of God and the great multitude.
- **Revelation 8–9**: the silence in heaven, the first six trumpets, the star fallen from heaven with the key to the pit of the abyss, the locusts, and the horsemen from the Euphrates.
- **Revelation 10**: the mighty angel with the little book and the seven thunders.
- **Revelation 12**: the woman clothed with the sun and the dragon cast down.
- **Revelation 13**: the beast from the sea, the beast from the earth, and the number 666.
- **Revelation 16**: the seven bowls, Har-magedon, the great earthquake and the hail.
- **Revelation 21**: the new heaven and the new earth, and the new Jerusalem coming down out of heaven.

Codex Vaticanus breaks off before Revelation, so the translation rests on Codex Sinaiticus and the 3rd-century papyrus P47. It keeps the grammatical irregularity of 1:4 ("from the one who is and who was and who is to come"), which echoes the divine name of Exodus 3:14, and renders the fourth horse of 6:8 "pale green", the literal colour of *chlōros*. The notes compare the throne room with Ezekiel 1, Daniel 7, Isaiah 6 and the Shi'ur Qomah, and the cosmic signs with Joel 2, the gospels and Qur'an 54:1. Chapters 5 and 7–21 translate the SBLGNT critical text and name, in the notes, the Textus Receptus wording behind the KJV wherever the canon's English edition quotes it (for example "I John" at 21:2). *Theos* is rendered "God" throughout, as in the canon's French. Verses with a note end in a bracketed marker `[ ]`; select it to open the note.
