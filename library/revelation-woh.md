The Book of Revelation is the last book of the New Testament, a series of visions given to John on the island of Patmos. The Raëlian canon discusses it at length in *The New Revelations*. This translation covers three chapters:

- **Revelation 1**: John's vision of one like a son of man among seven lampstands, whose voice is "like the sound of many waters", and who comes "with the clouds" (1:7).
- **Revelation 4**: a door opened in heaven, the throne, the twenty-four elders and the four living creatures full of eyes (lion, ox, man, eagle), crying "Holy, holy, holy".
- **Revelation 6**: the opening of the seals: the four horsemen, and at the sixth seal the sun turned black, the moon to blood and the stars falling to earth.

Codex Vaticanus breaks off before Revelation, so the translation rests on Codex Sinaiticus and the 3rd-century papyrus P47. It keeps the grammatical irregularity of 1:4 ("from the one who is and who was and who is to come"), which echoes the divine name of Exodus 3:14, and renders the fourth horse of 6:8 "pale green", the literal colour of *chlōros*. The notes compare the throne room with Ezekiel 1, Daniel 7, Isaiah 6 and the Shi'ur Qomah, and the cosmic signs with Joel 2, the gospels and Qur'an 54:1.
