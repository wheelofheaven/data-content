1 Kings (Hebrew *Melakhim*, "Kings") tells of Solomon's reign and the division of the kingdom, and of the prophets who confronted the kings of Israel. This translation covers the nine chapters that the Raëlian canon cites:

- **1 Kings 1–2**: Shelomoh (Solomon) made king; Adoniyahu (Adonijah) and Yo'av (Joab) at the horns of the altar
- **1 Kings 6**: the building of the house of YHWH
- **1 Kings 8**: the ark brought in, the cloud filling the house, and the prayer of Shelomoh
- **1 Kings 13**: the man of the Elohim from Yehudah and the torn altar at Beit-El (Bethel)
- **1 Kings 17**: Eliyahu (Elijah) fed by ravens; the widow of Tzarfat and her son
- **1 Kings 18**: the contest with the prophets of Ba'al on Mount Karmel and the fire of YHWH
- **1 Kings 19**: Eliyahu at Horeb, the still small voice, and the call of Elisha
- **1 Kings 22**: Mikhayehu's (Micaiah's) vision of YHWH enthroned among the host of the heavens

*The Book Which Tells the Truth* reads the cloud in the Temple, the fire on Karmel and the scene at Horeb as encounters with the Elohim. The translation renders the Hebrew plainly: *ʿărāp̄el* at 8:12 is "thick darkness", *qôl dəmāmâ daqqâ* at 19:12 is "a still small voice", and the canon's reading sits beside the traditional and critical readings in the notes.
