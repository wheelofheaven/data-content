The book of Job opens with a scene in the heavenly council: the "sons of the Elohim" (*bnei ha-elohim*) present themselves before YHWH, and among them comes *ha-satan*, "the adversary", who proposes to test Job. The book closes with YHWH answering Job out of the whirlwind, describing the making of the world, Behemoth and Leviathan, and with Job's restoration. This translation covers that frame and those speeches, chapters 1–2 and 38, 40–42, and Elihu's hymn to the storm in chapter 37, the thunder, snow and lightning of El.

The council scene matters for the Wheel of Heaven reading of the divine council, and the notes link the *bnei ha-elohim* to the same phrase in Genesis 6:2–4. *Ha-satan* is rendered "the adversary", a member of the council rather than the personal Satan of later tradition. The Hebrew uses "bless" as a scribal euphemism for "curse"; the translation writes "curse" where the sense requires it (1:11, 2:5, 2:9), and the notes explain the device. *Tam ve-yashar* is "blameless and upright". Verses with a note end in a bracketed marker `[ ]`; select it to open the note.
