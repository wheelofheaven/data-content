Hesiod's *Theogony* is the oldest systematic genealogy of the Greek gods. From **Chaos**, the primordial gap, and **Gaia** come the generations of **Ouranos** and **Kronos**. Kronos castrates his father and swallows his own children until **Zeus**, hidden on Crete, overthrows him in the war with the Titans, defeats **Typhoeus**, swallows **Metis**, and divides the honours of the settled order of the world. The poem also contains the Muses' saying about telling lies and truth, Prometheus' trick at Mekone and the making of the first woman, the oath of the gods sworn by the water of **Styx**, and the catalogues of divine marriages that close it.

For the Wheel of Heaven reading, the poem is the Greek witness to the pattern of generations of gods in conflict that the corpus follows through the Ugaritic, Akkadian and Hebrew texts. That comparison is made in the notes, not in the translation. The text is printed with the Greek, a Latin transliteration and the English side by side. The line notes explain the lexical decisions, the readings of the editors West and Most, where a word is a named god and where an ordinary noun, and Hesiod's etymologies and wordplay. Lines 929a–t, a second version of the Metis episode quoted by Chrysippus, are translated in place and explained in the notes. Lines with a note end in a bracketed marker `[ ]`; select it to open the note.
