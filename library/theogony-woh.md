**Theogony (Wheel of Heaven Translation)** is rendered from the polytonic
Greek text of the Perseus Digital Library digital edition
(canonical-greekLit tlg0020.tlg001.perseus-grc2) with the source, a Latin
transliteration, and the English translation displayed in parallel. It is
the Translation Program's first classical-Greek book.

Hesiod's poem is the oldest systematic genealogy of the Greek gods: from
**Chaos**, the primordial gap, and **Gaia** come the generations of
**Ouranos** and **Kronos**; Kronos unmans his father and swallows his own
children until **Zeus**, hidden on Krete, overthrows him in the
**Titanomachy**, defeats **Typhoeus**, swallows **Metis**, and distributes
the honours of the settled cosmic order. Along the way the poem carries
the Muses' famous declaration about falsehood and truth, the Prometheus
affair at Mekone with the fashioning of the first woman (the "beautiful
evil"), the oath of the gods sworn by the water of **Styx**, and the
catalogues of divine marriages that close the composition. For the Wheel
of Heaven frame the poem is the Greek anchor of the succession-conflict
and theomachy pattern the corpus tracks across the Ugaritic, Akkadian,
and Hebrew material — a convergence documented in the commentary
apparatus, never imported into the translation surface.

**Lines with commentary carry a bracketed marker `[ ]` at the end of the
translated line. Click the marker to open the commentary panel.** Per-line
commentary documents lexical decisions, West's and Most's philological
options, the personified-versus-common-noun boundaries, the poem's
folk-etymologies and wordplay, and the labeled comparative links to the
wider corpus.

The complete composition — all 1022 lines plus the 929a–t variant passage
(a doublet of the Metis episode preserved by Chrysippus, translated in
place with its editorial status documented in the commentary) — was
translated in four sequential blocks and signed off as **complete** as of
2026-08-04.

The Wheel of Heaven English translation, per-line commentary, and glossary
are released CC0. The underlying Greek text is the Perseus Digital Library
digital edition, CC BY-SA 4.0 (print basis: Evelyn-White, *Hesiod, the
Homeric Hymns and Homerica*, Loeb Classical Library, 1914, Public Domain);
