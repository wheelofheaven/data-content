The Wisdom of Solomon is a Greek book of the Septuagint, written in Alexandria in the first century BCE and placed among the Old Testament books in the Catholic and Orthodox canons. This translation covers the two chapters that *The Book Which Tells the Truth* quotes, at 6:22 and at 13:1 and 13:5:

- **6:1–21**: an address to kings: wisdom, "radiant and unfading", is easily seen by those who love her
- **6:22–25**: "what wisdom is, and how she came to be, I will declare, and I will not hide mysteries from you"
- **13:1–9**: people who did not come to know "him who is" from the good things that are seen, though "from the greatness and beauty of created things their maker is seen by analogy"
- **13:10–19**: the woodcutter who carves an idol from a scrap of timber and prays to it

It is the corpus's first book with no Hebrew original. Verses with a note end in a bracketed marker `[ ]`; select it to open the note.
