The **Gospel of Thomas** is a collection of 114 sayings attributed to Jesus, introduced as "the hidden sayings that the living Jesus spoke, and Didymos Judas Thomas wrote them down." It has no narrative of Jesus' birth, miracles, death or resurrection. The complete text survives only in Coptic, in the second tractate of Nag Hammadi Codex II, a fourth-century book found in Upper Egypt in 1945; three Greek papyri from Oxyrhynchus (P.Oxy. 1, 654 and 655) preserve parts of an earlier Greek form. The manuscript closes with the title *The Gospel according to Thomas*.

About half of the sayings have parallels in the gospels of Matthew, Mark and Luke, such as the sower, the mustard seed and the lost sheep; the others have none, among them the kingdom that is "inside you, and it is outside you" (saying 3), the lion that "will become human" (saying 7) and the five trees in Paradise (saying 19). Scholars disagree on the text's date and on its relation to the canonical gospels: some place its core in the first century, independent of them, others in the second century and dependent on them.

This translation follows the Coptic manuscript, including at the hard sayings where editors often emend; the emendations and the Greek readings are given in the notes. Square brackets mark words standing on letters restored by modern editors in damaged places, angle brackets mark a word supplied where the Coptic has none, and "(?)" marks a word whose meaning is uncertain. Sayings with a note end in a bracketed marker `[ ]`; select it to open the note.
