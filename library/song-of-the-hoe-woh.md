*The Song of the Hoe* is a Sumerian hymn. It opens with **Enlil** resolving to make the seed of the land sprout from the ground: he separates heaven from earth, fixes the bond of heaven and earth (*Dur-an-ki*), and at **Uzu-e-a**, "where flesh came forth", sets the hoe (*al*) to the soil, so that the first humans break through the ground like plants and are placed in the brick-mould. The rest of the poem praises the hoe as the tool that builds cities, temples and the whole order of civilization, threading the syllable *al* through scores of words.

The hymn belongs with *Enki and Ninmah* and *Atra-ḫasīs*, where humans are made to relieve the gods of toil, and with Genesis 2:7, where the human (*ʾadam*) is formed from the ground (*ʾadamah*). The line notes explain the lexical decisions, the ETCSL editors' options and the *al* wordplay. Lines with a note end in a bracketed marker `[ ]`; select it to open the note.
