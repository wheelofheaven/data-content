The Book of Jubilees retells Genesis and the opening of Exodus as a revelation dictated to Moses on Sinai by the angel of the presence, with history divided into periods of forty-nine years (jubilees). It was widely read at Qumran, where more than fifteen copies were found, and it is part of the Bible of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church and of the Beta Israel. This translation covers chapters 1–12, from Moses' ascent of Sinai to Abraham's youth in Ur.

Along the way Jubilees adds much that Genesis does not say. Enoch is the first human to learn writing and the scribe who records the judgment of the Watchers (4:17–26). The Watchers' descent is told in brief (chapter 5). Noah's covenant includes a ban on eating blood, and chapter 6 defends a year of exactly 364 days against the lunar calendar ("they will make the moon disturb the seasons", 6:36). Mastema, prince of the spirits, appears by name and is allowed to keep a tenth of the demons (10:7–9). A wind throws down the tower of Babel (10:21). The young Abram burns the house of idols (12:12–14) and is taught Hebrew, "the language of creation" (12:25–26).

The translation keeps Mastema as a proper name rather than "Satan", and "heavenly tablets" and "angel of the presence" as fixed phrases. It keeps the calendar polemic in its own voice. Readings that connect the Watchers or Mastema with the Elohim are set out in the notes. Verses with a note end in a bracketed marker `[ ]`; select it to open the note.

See also [1 Enoch](/library/book-of-enoch-woh/), [Daniel](/library/daniel-woh/), [Genesis](/library/genesis-woh/) and the [Epic of Gilgamesh](/library/gilgamesh-woh/).
