*Enki and Ninmah* is a Sumerian account of how humans were made. In the time before the flood the lesser gods toil at digging canals. Namma, the primordial mother, brings their complaint to Enki, who directs that clay from above the *abzu* (the fresh-water deep) be kneaded and shaped into humans to bear the gods' labor. Enki and the goddess Ninmah then compete at a feast: she shapes bodies with defects, and he finds a place in society for each of them.

The text is the Mesopotamian counterpart to the making of humans in Genesis 1–2, and shares the motif of humans created to relieve the gods' toil with *Atra-ḫasīs*. The line notes explain the lexical decisions and the ETCSL editors' options, and point to parallels in Genesis 1–2, Job 10:8–9, *Atra-ḫasīs* and *Enūma Eliš* VI.
