Mark 16 tells of the women who find Jesus' tomb empty. It is also the best-known textual problem in the New Testament. In the two oldest complete Greek manuscripts, Codex Vaticanus and Codex Sinaiticus, the gospel ends at verse 8 with the women running away, "for they were afraid". The "longer ending" (verses 9–20), with the resurrection appearances, the signs that will follow believers (handling snakes and drinking poison unharmed, 16:18) and the Ascension (16:19), is missing there and in some early translations, but present in Codex Alexandrinus, Codex Bezae and the later Byzantine text. Most modern critical scholars regard it as a 2nd-century addition.

This translation gives verses 9–20 as part of the chapter, as they stand in the received text, and explains in the notes where they are missing and why scholars date them later. The Raëlian canon discusses verses 18 and 19 in *Extraterrestrials Took Me To Their Planet*. Verses with a note end in a bracketed marker `[ ]`; select it to open the note.
