The book of Daniel is the main apocalypse of the Hebrew Bible. Chapters 1–6 are court tales set in Babylon: the four-metal statue and the stone cut without hands, the fiery furnace, Nebuchadnezzar's madness, the writing on the wall at Belshazzar's feast, and the lions' den. Chapters 7–12 are visions: the four beasts and the Ancient of Days on a throne of fire, with "one like a son of man" coming with the clouds of heaven (7:9–14); the ram and the goat; the seventy weeks (9); the angel by the Tigris and the wars of the kings of the north and south (10–11); and the resurrection of the dead (12:2).

Daniel is written in two languages, Hebrew (1:1–2:4a and 8–12) and Aramaic (2:4b–7:28). The translation marks the language of every verse. The Dead Sea Scrolls show that this two-language form is old.

The translation leaves open several readings that later tradition settled. "One like a son of man" (7:13) and "an anointed one" (9:25–26) stay lowercase and indefinite. "Ancient of Days" is kept as a title. MENE MENE TEKEL UPHARSIN stays in Aramaic. The seventy weeks are not converted into years, and the 1,290 and 1,335 days of 12:11–12 are left as written. Chapter 11 describes the persecution under Antiochus IV accurately up to verse 39, then predicts a death in Egypt that did not happen; scholars use this to date the book to 167–164 BCE, and the translation leaves the prediction as it stands. Christian and Raëlian readings of these passages are discussed in the notes. Verses with a note end in a bracketed marker `[ ]`; select it to open the note.
