1 Enoch is a collection of Jewish writings from the last centuries BCE, attributed to Enoch, the seventh patriarch after Adam. *Yes... I am Raelian* names it among the texts "closer to the truth", alongside the Mahābhārata, the Kojiki, the Popol Vuh and the Epic of Gilgamesh. This translation covers its two oldest parts.

**The Book of the Watchers (chapters 1–36)** expands Genesis 6:1–4. Two hundred "sons of heaven" led by Šemiḥazah descend on Mount Hermon, take human wives and father the giants. Asael teaches humans metalwork, weapons, cosmetics and the reading of the stars (8:1–3), the passage the Raëlian canon cites most. Enoch then intercedes for the Watchers, sees the heavenly throne (chapter 14), and is taken on journeys through the ends of the earth, the places of the dead, the tree of life and the valley of Hinnom (Gehenna). Jude 14–15 quotes 1 Enoch 1:9 word for word.

**The Astronomical Book (chapters 72–82)** is the angel Uriel's account of the courses of the sun, moon and stars. It argues for a year of exactly 364 days (72:32) and warns that people err when they fail to count the four extra days (75:2). The same calendar is defended in Jubilees and was followed at Qumran. The Aramaic fragments of this book are older and longer than the Ethiopic text that survives complete.

The translation keeps the Aramaic forms of the angels' names (Šemiḥazah, ʿAśāʾēl and others), keeps textual differences between the witnesses visible (for example four archangels in the Aramaic of chapter 20 and seven in the Greek), and does not harmonize the astronomical figures. The reading of the Watchers as the Elohim is set out in the notes; the translation follows the mainstream scholarly reading. Verses with a note end in a bracketed marker `[ ]`; select it to open the note.

See also [Jubilees](/library/jubilees-woh/), [Daniel](/library/daniel-woh/), [Genesis](/library/genesis-woh/) and the [Epic of Gilgamesh](/library/gilgamesh-woh/).
