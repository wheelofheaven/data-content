Luke 21 is Luke's version of Jesus' discourse on the destruction of the temple and the end of the age. The Raëlian canon discusses two passages from it in *The End of the World*: the signs "in sun and moon and stars" with "distress of nations in perplexity" (21:25–26), and "the Son of Man coming in a cloud" (21:27). This translation renders the chapter from the oldest Greek witnesses, including P75, the earliest substantial manuscript of Luke.

The notes trace the cosmic signs back to Joel 2 and forward to Mark 13, Acts 2 and Revelation 6, and compare the Qur'an's "splitting of the moon" (54:1). They point out that Luke has a single cloud (*nephelē*) where Mark and Matthew have clouds, linking the passage to the cloud of the Ascension in Acts 1:9–11, and they set out the scholarly readings of "this generation will not pass away" (21:32).
