**Infinity** is the foundational cosmological framework of the Wheel of Heaven corpus. It holds that **the cosmos is infinite along three mutually reinforcing dimensions**: infinite in **spatial extent** (no boundary, no edge, no center), infinite in **temporal extent** (no beginning, no end), and infinite in **hierarchical scale** (no smallest scale, no largest scale). Its source warrant is the Yahweh-delivered passages in *The Book Which Tells the Truth* (1974) and *Extra-Terrestrials Took Me to Their Planet* (1975), developed through the Outro Section II of *timeline.epub*. Within it, **Fractal Cosmology** is the spatial dimension, the **{% wiki(slug="mass-effect") %}Mass Effect{% end %}** the temporal-scaling dimension, the **Cosmic Chain** the causal-cosmic-civilizational dimension, and the **Four Levels** the ethical-practical correlate.

The framework is structurally distinct from mainstream Big Bang cosmology, which holds the universe **finite in age** (about 13.8 billion years), **finite in observable extent** (the cosmic horizon), and **homogeneous-and-isotropic** at large scales (the cosmological principle). Infinity rejects each: the cosmos is infinite in age (the Big Bang being at most the start of our observable region, not of being itself), infinite in extent (the cosmic horizon bounding our observation, not being itself), and **fractal-hierarchical-self-similar** rather than homogeneous, each scale revealing further structure rather than smoothing into uniformity. It is therefore not a refinement of mainstream cosmology but a structurally different position.

That content carries across domains. **Cosmologically**, it recasts the standard questions of Big Bang cosmology — what preceded the Big Bang? what lies beyond the observable universe? what is the smallest scale? — as questions whose presuppositions do not match the cosmos, structurally like asking "what is north of the North Pole?": well-formed but referentially empty. **Theologically**, it rejects the personal deity who exists "somewhere" and intervenes "from somewhere"; in an infinite cosmos with no center and no boundary, no location can be that deity's locus. **Ethically**, it grounds the **Four Levels** of consideration — every situation weighed against four distinct levels: the Infinite, the Elohim, human society, and the individual self.

The reading is source-grounded. The Raëlian material articulates infinity explicitly across the "Mankind: A Disease of the Universe" passage in *The Book Which Tells the Truth*, the "Neither God nor Soul" section of *Extra-Terrestrials Took Me to Their Planet* (1975), and the "Who Created the Creator of the Creators?" FAQ in *Let's Welcome the Extra-terrestrials* (1979). The three-dimensional articulation and its integration with the Fractal Cosmology, Mass Effect, Cosmic Chain, and Four Levels entries is corpus development beyond what the source directly provides, though anchored in it — a status of source-grounding with corpus systematic extension.

## Conventional understanding

As a cosmological-philosophical position, Infinity overlaps with several mainstream-scholarly positions while extending beyond what science and philosophy have established.

### Mathematical infinity

Mainstream mathematics has developed infinity over several centuries.

**Potential vs. actual infinity**. Aristotle distinguished potential infinity (an unending process extendable indefinitely) from actual infinity (a completed totality of infinitely many elements), and held that only potential infinity is legitimate, actual infinity being incoherent — the dominant view through most of the medieval and early modern periods.

**Cantorian transfinite hierarchy**. Georg Cantor's work in the 1870s–1880s established set theory's framework for actual infinity, showing that infinite sets can have different sizes (cardinalities):

- **Aleph-null (ℵ₀)**: the cardinality of countably infinite sets (natural numbers, rational numbers, algebraic numbers)
- **Aleph-one (ℵ₁)**: the cardinality of the smallest uncountable set
- **The continuum (𝔠 = 2^ℵ₀)**: the cardinality of the real numbers
- **The continuum hypothesis**: whether 𝔠 = ℵ₁, shown to be independent of standard ZFC set theory (Gödel 1940, Cohen 1963)

The hierarchy continues indefinitely through successively larger infinities.

**Set-theoretic paradoxes**. Russell's paradox (1901) — "the set of all sets that do not contain themselves" — showed naive set theory to be inconsistent, prompting axiomatic set theory (ZFC and alternatives); Burali-Forti's paradox, Cantor's paradox, and others exposed further problems in the foundations.

**Hilbert's hotel**. David Hilbert's thought experiment (1924) illustrates actual infinity: a hotel with infinitely many rooms, all occupied, still accommodates any finite number of new guests by shifting occupants to higher-numbered rooms, and even countably infinite new guests by moving them to even-numbered rooms — infinite sets behaving unlike finite ones.

**Intuitionist vs. classical infinity**. The intuitionist tradition (Brouwer, Heyting, others) rejects actual infinity for potential infinity, treating statements about actual infinity as meaningless or as referring only to the constructive-finite procedures that generate the objects; the classical tradition (Hilbert, Bourbaki, the mainstream) accepts it as foundational. The two produce different theories, with some theorems valid in one but not the other.

Infinity overlaps mathematical infinity but is categorically distinct from it: it operates at the **physical-cosmological level**, not the **mathematical-set-theoretic level**, making a claim about the structure of the cosmos rather than about cardinality. The Cantorian hierarchy is one articulation of infinity within the framework, but the framework's content extends well beyond the mathematical dimension.

### Cosmological infinity debates

Mainstream cosmology has worked on infinity across several positions.

**The classical Newtonian framework**. Newtonian cosmology assumed a universe infinite in space (no boundary, edge, or center) and in time (no beginning or end, indefinite past and future) — the mainstream position from about 1687 (Newton's *Principia*) to about 1920.

**Olbers's paradox**. Heinrich Wilhelm Olbers's paradox (1823, on earlier formulations) troubled the infinite-static universe: with stars distributed roughly uniformly through an infinite, static, eternal cosmos, every line of sight should meet a star and the night sky should blaze — yet it is dark. Different frameworks resolve it differently: Big Bang through finite age, fractal cosmology through hierarchical structure, intergalactic absorption in part.

**Steady-state cosmology**. Steady-state cosmology (Hoyle, Bondi, Gold, 1948) proposed an infinite-eternal universe with continuous matter creation holding density constant across cosmological time. Observations across the 1960s–1970s refuted it — the cosmic microwave background, the redshift-distance relation, primordial nucleosynthesis.

**Big Bang cosmology**. Since about 1965 the mainstream has been Big Bang cosmology, holding the universe:

- **Finite in age**: approximately 13.8 billion years since the Big Bang event
- **Finite in observable extent**: the observable universe approximately 93 billion light-years in diameter, set by the speed of light and the time elapsed since the Big Bang
- **Possibly infinite in spatial extent**: beyond the observable universe, the spatial extent is undecidable from our position; mainstream cosmology takes various positions on whether the broader extent is infinite or finite
- **Homogeneous and isotropic at large scales**: the cosmological principle, no preferred locations or directions at sufficiently large scales

Infinity diverges structurally: it rejects the finite age, treats the cosmic horizon as the boundary of our observation rather than of being itself, and rejects the cosmological principle for fractal-hierarchical-self-similar structure (see [Fractal Cosmology](../fractal-cosmology/)).

**Modern multiverse proposals**. Contemporary proposals include eternal inflation (Linde, Vilenkin, others), the string-theory landscape (Susskind, others), many-worlds quantum mechanics (Everett interpretation), and cyclic cosmologies (Penrose's conformal cyclic model, Steinhardt-Turok). Infinity is distinct from each: its structure is hierarchical-self-similar rather than parallel-multi-universe (see [Fractal Cosmology](../fractal-cosmology/)).

**The contested cosmological principle**. The cosmological principle (homogeneity-and-isotropy at large scales) is the mainstream assumption Infinity rejects. It has been contested by Pietronero, Coleman-Pietronero, Baryshev, Nottale, and others, and whether galaxy-distribution data at the largest scales is fractal or transitions to homogeneity-and-isotropy remains debated.

### Philosophical infinity treatments

Mainstream philosophy has worked on infinity across several traditions.

**Aristotelian potential vs. actual infinity**. Aristotle's view that only potential infinity is legitimate, with actual infinity incoherent, was foundational through most of the medieval and early modern periods. His argument: actual infinity would require completing an infinite process, impossible by definition; only potential infinity — an unending, indefinitely extendable process — is genuinely conceivable.

**Kantian antinomies**. Immanuel Kant's *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781, 1787) treated the antinomies of pure reason, including those over the world's infinity in space and time: equally compelling arguments support both the thesis (the world has a beginning in time and a limit in space) and the antithesis (it has neither), each refuting the other, which places the question beyond the legitimate scope of pure reason. Kant's resolution: the world-in-itself has neither finite nor infinite extent in the sense at issue; the question is ill-posed for the world-in-itself, though well-posed for phenomena within experience.

**The continental-tradition treatments**. Hegel, Husserl, Heidegger, and others in the continental tradition treated infinity in distinct frameworks: Hegel's "true infinite" versus "bad infinite"; Husserl's phenomenology of horizon and infinite open-endedness; Heidegger's engagement with finitude and the question of being.

**Analytic-tradition treatments**. The analytic tradition has worked on infinity through philosophy of mathematics (Frege, Russell, Quine, others), philosophy of cosmology (Earman, Norton, others), and metaphysics, generally toward set-theoretic articulations complementing the mathematical scholarship.

Infinity partly overlaps these treatments but articulates its own position, drawing on the traditions while extending through the cosmological-civilizational content the source articulates.

### Theological infinity treatments

Mainstream theology has worked on infinity and the divine across many traditions; the detailed treatment is in *Comparative observations* below. Infinity is structurally distinct from these but has points of contact: it rejects the supernatural-deity framework that conflates infinity with a personal deity, while paralleling the apophatic-mystical, pantheist, and panentheist traditions that approach infinity as cosmic-impersonal.

### The "Mankind: A Disease of the Universe" passage in *The Book Which Tells the Truth*

The first passage establishing the framework is in *The Book Which Tells the Truth* (1974), the chapter "Mankind: A Disease of the Universe":

> *"Progress continues, and our own research continues for the purpose of understanding and relating to the large being of whom we are a part, and on whose atoms we are parasites. These atoms are the planets and the stars."*

> *"In fact, we have been able to discover that intelligent living beings in the infinitely small live on particles that are planets and suns to them. They ask the same questions as ourselves."*

> *"Humanity is a disease inside this gigantic being, and the planets and stars are its atoms. Also this same gigantic being is in its turn a parasite on other greater atoms. In both directions, it is infinite. But the important thing is to make sure that the disease, which is humanity, continues to exist and never dies."*

The passage establishes several components:

**1. The infinite hierarchical structure**. *"In both directions, it is infinite."* The cosmos extends infinitely upward and downward, with intelligent civilizations distributed across the hierarchy.

**2. The "disease" framing**. The "humanity is a disease" formulation is **anti-anthropocentric**, not pessimistic. It registers humanity — and intelligent civilizations generally, the Elohim included at their scale — as a *small* phenomenon within the cosmos, like a microscopic organism within a much larger one. This is what the **First Level of the Four Levels framework** states: *"Our life means nothing when compared with the Infinite."* It is a matter of scale-perspective, not a value judgment.

**3. The continuity-imperative**. *"The important thing is to make sure that the disease, which is humanity, continues to exist and never dies."* Despite humanity's small cosmic position, the continuity of human civilization — and of intelligent civilizations generally — matters within the cosmic-creative cycle (see [Cosmic Chain](../cosmic-chain/)).

**4. The Elohim's parallel position**. *"This same gigantic being is in its turn a parasite on other greater atoms."* The Elohim occupy a scale within the infinite hierarchy, small within their own larger beings. The same cosmological situation applies to humans and to the Elohim alike.

## The concept's content

### The three principal dimensions of infinity

Infinity runs along three mutually reinforcing dimensions.

#### 1. Spatial infinity

The cosmos extends indefinitely in space. There is **no boundary** — any scale proposed as the largest sits within a larger one, and the observable-universe boundary (set by the speed of light and the time since whatever local event let us begin observing) bounds our observation, not being itself. There is **no center**: *"Since the universe is infinite, it cannot have a center"* — center is defined against a bounded region, and with no findable boundary no location qualifies. There is **no edge** separating the cosmos from any external region; it is the structure within which everything exists, extending in every direction without findable termination. Within this dimension the Fractal Cosmology framework's spatial-self-similar structure operates (see [Fractal Cosmology](../fractal-cosmology/)).

#### 2. Temporal infinity

The cosmos extends indefinitely in time. There is **no beginning** — any era proposed as the first is preceded by earlier ones, and the Big Bang (about 13.8 billion years before the present) is at most the beginning of our observable region, not of being itself. There is **no end**: proposed future scenarios — heat death, big crunch, big rip, others — are at most the future of our observable region, not the end of being. Matter is **eternally conserved** — *"Nothing is lost, nothing is created, everything is transformed"* — matter and energy eternal, only their forms changing; the structure continues in both past and future without findable termination. Within this dimension the Mass Effect framework's time-scaling content operates (see [Mass Effect](../mass-effect/)), as does the Cosmic Chain's creator-recursion content (see [Cosmic Chain](../cosmic-chain/)).

#### 3. Hierarchical infinity

The cosmos extends indefinitely in scale. There is **no smallest scale**: the atoms of our bodies contain, on the source's claim, intelligent beings whose worlds we cannot perceive, whose atoms contain smaller beings still, without termination. There is **no largest scale**: the Earth is a particle of an atom of an atom of the hand of a gigantic being, itself one figure in a vastly larger world containing yet more gigantic beings, without termination. The same process runs upward and downward — no privileged scale, no asymmetry — symmetric across the infinite hierarchy, framing both the Fractal Cosmology framework's spatial-self-similar content and the Mass Effect framework's time-scaling content.

### The integration of the three dimensions

The three dimensions work as a unified framework, not as independent properties. Space and time are symmetric, both extending without findable beginning or end — *"It is as foolish to search for the beginning of the universe in time as it is to search for the beginning of space"* — and the hierarchical structure operates across all spatial and temporal extent, the same at every cosmic moment and location. None is privileged or reducible to the others; each is a real feature of the cosmos. This integrated three-dimensional infinity is the framework within which Fractal Cosmology, Mass Effect, Cosmic Chain, and Four Levels operate.

### The principal cosmological consequences

Infinity carries cosmological consequences across several domains.

**The reframing of cosmic-origin questions**. "What was there before the Big Bang?" and "what initiated the cosmos?" rest on a presupposition the cosmos does not meet. There is no cosmic origin to find; the cosmos has always existed and always will, our observable region being one phase within the infinite structure.

**The reframing of cosmic-boundary questions**. "What lies beyond the observable universe?" and "what is the spatial extent of the cosmos?" fare the same. There is no cosmic boundary; the observable universe bounds our observation, not being itself.

**The reframing of fundamental-physics questions**. "What is the smallest scale of physical organization?" and "what are the fundamental particles?" have provisional, not absolute, answers. The currently "fundamental" particles (quarks, leptons, gauge bosons, the Higgs boson) are fundamental from our vantage; deeper investigation presumably reveals further sub-structure without end.

**The reframing of cosmic-uniqueness questions**. "Is humanity unique in the cosmos?" and "are we the only intelligent civilization?" have a clear answer: no. Intelligent civilizations exist at every scale of the infinite hierarchy; ours is one of countless such civilizations, which deflates the traditional anthropocentric claims about humanity's cosmic significance.

### The principal theological consequences

Infinity carries theological consequences the source articulates explicitly.

**The rejection of conventional supernatural deity**. *"No argument aiming to support the existence of any type of god or soul can be sustained when we glimpse, however briefly, at the infinite nature of the universe. No heaven could exist in any particular place because, since the universe is infinite, it cannot have a center."* No location can be a personal deity's locus in an infinite cosmos with no center and no boundary.

**The two senses of "God" within the framework**. The source's distinction:

> *"It all depends on what we mean by the word God. If we are thinking of Infinity, then yes, but only in part... If we mean by 'God' a being having power over us, not at all, because there is no such 'God'."*

If "God" means **infinity** itself — the cosmic structure containing all scales, beings, and events — it exists in that cosmic-impersonal sense, which the pantheist and panentheist traditions parallel; if "God" means a **personal being who has power over us**, no such God exists.

**The "infinitely indifferent" cosmos**. *"The Infinite is omnipresent, and we are a part of it as it is a part of us. But it has no power over us, and it is 'infinitely' indifferent to our decisions or our behavior."* Not hostile but non-attentive — unaware of events at our scale as we are unaware of events on the atoms of our hands.

**The Elohim as not-divine**. *"If by 'God' one means a personal being who created us and who cares about us, then there are such beings — the Elohim, our specific creators, who do indeed care about us in the concrete sense the corpus has been describing — but these beings are not God in the traditional theological sense. They are advanced beings of our own kind, themselves created by other beings, themselves operating within the larger infinite cosmos that contains them and us together."* They are creators in the operational sense — they designed and produced our biological lineage — but finite beings within the infinite cosmos, subject to the same structure.

### The Four Levels as ethical correlate

Infinity's ethical correlate is the **Four Levels**, the source's practical-ethical orientation:

**Level 1: The Infinite**. *"Our life means nothing when compared with the Infinite."* Our lives are vanishingly insignificant within the cosmos, which gives cosmic perspective: we are not responsible for the cosmos and it does not depend on us, so we can act at our own scale without cosmic responsibility crushing the possibility of action.

**Level 2: The Elohim**. *"Our life is very important, because we are their children, and we must show them that we are proud of having been privileged enough to have been created in their image."* Relational accountability: our actions matter because they matter to the beings who made us and who watch what we do — not cosmic significance, which dissolved at Level 1.

**Level 3: Human society**. *"In relation to human society, our life is equally very important, because we are the result of a long list of survivors who have escaped the epidemics and wars which have made us the offspring of a long natural selection. We owe it to ourselves to participate actively in the plan that will allow humanity to reach the Golden Age, which it greatly deserves and which it is about to enter. We are the cells of this huge being that is Humanity."* Collective purpose: our actions help or hinder the human story our ancestors built and our descendants inherit.

**Level 4: The individual self**. *"Finally, in relation to our own self, our life has only the importance that we give it."* Personal meaning-making: the cosmos imposes no meaning, so we choose what ours mean.

**The integrated framework**. The four levels function together as a structured framework for ethical orientation, not as four competing frameworks. Living well requires holding all four at once:

- Level 1 perspective when personal anxiety threatens to crush action
- Level 2 accountability when nihilism threatens to dissolve commitment
- Level 3 purpose when individual concerns threaten to overwhelm broader responsibilities
- Level 4 meaning-making when impersonal frameworks threaten to extinguish individual significance

The Four Levels is the practical-ethical orientation Infinity grounds. Each level is treated in the dedicated [Four Levels](../four-levels/) entry when written; the Infinity entry establishes the foundational cosmological framework within which they operate.

### The "love" constant across all four levels

The source registers **love** as the constant across all four levels: *"The most important level is that relating to infinity, for it is in relation to this level that all things must be judged - but always with one constant factor: love."* Love is the integrative principle connecting the levels:

- Love of infinity (the cosmic-impersonal level)
- Love of the Elohim (the creator-relational level)
- Love of humanity (the collective level)
- Love of the individual (the personal level)

Love is the ethical content Infinity grounds at all four levels at once — the practical orientation fitted to the infinite-cosmological structure.

## Modern reinterpretations

### Mathematical infinity history

Beyond the treatment above: Aristotle's restriction to potential infinity dominated until the late nineteenth century. **Georg Cantor**'s diagonal argument (1870s–1880s) showed the real numbers uncountably infinite while the natural numbers are only countably infinite, and he developed the transfinite ordinal and cardinal hierarchies; his continuum hypothesis was shown consistent with ZFC by **Kurt Gödel** (1940) and independent of it by **Paul Cohen** (1963). Among the paradoxes, Russell's (1901) showed naive set theory inconsistent, the Burali-Forti paradox (1897) concerns the ordinal of all ordinals, and Cantor's paradox the cardinality of all cardinals — prompting axiomatic set theory. **David Hilbert**'s hotel (1924) also illustrates the Galileo paradox, that the even numbers are equinumerous with all the natural numbers. The intuitionists **Brouwer, Heyting, Bishop** rejected actual infinity, where the classical mainstream (Hilbert, Bourbaki) accepts it. The framework itself operates at the physical-cosmological rather than the set-theoretic level; whether its cosmological infinity corresponds to particular Cantorian cardinalities is an open question the corpus does not resolve.

### Cosmological infinity debates

Beyond the treatment above: the classical Newtonian infinite-static universe faced gravitational instability as well as Olbers's paradox; steady-state cosmology (Hoyle, Bondi, Gold) ran from 1948 to 1965; and Big Bang cosmology (Lemaître, Gamow, Penzias-Wilson, others) became mainstream from about 1965, its finite age, finite observable extent, and undecidable extent beyond the observable universe structurally distinct from Infinity's hierarchical-self-similar structure. The contested cosmological principle has been challenged by Pietronero, Coleman-Pietronero, Baryshev, Nottale, and others; the fractal-cosmology debates are treated in the [Fractal Cosmology](../fractal-cosmology/) entry.

### Philosophical infinity treatments

Beyond the treatment above, **G. W. F. Hegel**'s *Science of Logic* (1812–1816) set the "true infinite" (the self-relating absolute) against the "bad infinite" (the indefinitely-repeating finite), which the framework reads as not directly mapping onto its cosmological content. Contemporary continental work — Meillassoux's speculative realism, Harman's object-oriented ontology, others — has treated cosmic infinity in distinct frameworks.

### Sendy on infinity

**Jean Sendy**, in *Ces dieux qui firent le ciel et la terre* (1969) and *L'ère du Verseau* (1970), develops complementary content on infinity:

**Cosmic-civilizational infinity**. Sendy treats the cosmos as holding intelligent civilizations at multiple scales, his Galaxiens framework operating within the infinite cosmic structure.

**The Gamow logarithmic-scale anchor**. Sendy draws on George Gamow's scientific-popularization device placing humans roughly midway between the atom and the Sun on a logarithmic scale of physical magnitudes. The detailed treatment lives in the [Fractal Cosmology](../fractal-cosmology/) entry.

**The space-heterogeneity content**. Sendy treats space as heterogeneous rather than homogeneous, with cosmological implications. The detailed treatment lives in the [Fractal Cosmology](../fractal-cosmology/) entry.

The framework overlaps Sendy's position and extends it.

**Mauro Biglino** and **Paul Anthony Wallis** have engaged the framework little. Their work on the Hebrew Bible and the alliance-mediated history is structurally compatible with it, but neither engages its distinctive cosmological content directly.

Infinity aligns with mainstream mathematical infinity technically while operating at the physical-cosmological level; is distinct from Big Bang cosmology while accepting its observations within their empirical domain; aligns with the contested-cosmological-principle scholarship and with the Kantian, Hegelian, phenomenological, and analytic traditions, extending beyond them through its cosmological-civilizational content; and aligns with Sendy on cosmic-civilizational infinity and the Gamow logarithmic-scale anchor.

## Comparative observations

Infinity parallels cosmic-infinity content in cultural-religious traditions worldwide.

### Hindu Brahman and Atman infinity

The Hindu tradition develops cosmic infinity through the Brahman/Atman framework.

**Brahman as infinite cosmic-impersonal reality**. The Vedic and Vedantic tradition treats *Brahman* as the infinite cosmic-impersonal reality that pervades and constitutes all existence — *neti neti* ("not this, not this"), beyond all predicates and descriptions. It parallels Infinity's cosmic-impersonal content.

**Atman as identical with Brahman**. The Upanishadic teaching *tat tvam asi* ("that thou art") and *aham brahmāsmi* ("I am Brahman") identifies the individual atman with the cosmic Brahman — parallel to Infinity's "we are part of infinity, and infinity is part of us."

**The Brahmanda nested universes**. The Hindu cosmology of countless brahmandas (cosmic eggs), each a complete universe, parallels Infinity's hierarchical-cosmic content (see [Fractal Cosmology](../fractal-cosmology/)).

The source's "those who think of 'God' as Infinity, as most oriental religions teach, are right" passage explicitly identifies the Hindu content with the framework's cosmic-impersonal content.

### Buddhist śūnyatā and infinity

The Buddhist tradition develops cosmic emptiness and infinity.

**Śūnyatā** (emptiness) articulates the absence of inherent self-existence in any phenomenon — the Buddhist form of the cosmic-impersonal foundational reality Infinity articulates through different content. The Madhyamaka school (Nāgārjuna, Candrakīrti, others) developed it across the second–eighth centuries CE.

**Buddhist cosmic infinity**. Buddhist cosmology holds infinite cosmic time (the *kalpa* cosmology of vast cyclic time scales) and infinite cosmic space (many cosmic systems). The temporal content is treated in the [Mass Effect](../mass-effect/) entry; the spatial content parallels Infinity's spatial-infinity content.

**Indra's net**. The *Avataṃsaka Sūtra*'s Indra's net image articulates cosmic interconnection across the cosmic structure. The detailed treatment lives in the [Fractal Cosmology](../fractal-cosmology/) entry.

### Jain ananta cosmology

The Jain tradition develops cosmic infinity through the *ananta* (infinite, endless) framework.

**The Jain cosmic infinity**. Jain cosmology holds the cosmos infinite in space (the loka extending indefinitely) and in time (cosmic cycles across vast time scales).

**The Jain ananta categories**. Jain philosophy distinguishes several categories of ananta across its framework, parallel to Infinity within its own framing.

### Jewish Ein Sof Kabbalistic infinity

The Jewish-mystical Kabbalistic tradition develops cosmic infinity through the **Ein Sof** framework.

**Ein Sof as the infinite divine**. *Ein Sof* (literally "without end" or "infinite") is the infinite divine reality beyond all predicates and descriptions — the cosmic-impersonal foundational reality from which the Sephirotic emanations proceed.

**The relationship between Ein Sof and the Sephirot**. The Sephirot are emanations from Ein Sof; the cosmic structure runs through them while grounded in the Ein Sof reality.

**The Lurianic Kabbalistic development**. **Isaac Luria** (1534–1572) added the *tzimtzum* — the divine self-contraction creating space for finite existence within Ein Sof — paralleling Infinity's cosmic-impersonal content within Jewish-mystical framing.

### Christian apophatic theology

The Christian tradition develops the divine infinite through the **apophatic** (or *via negativa*) tradition.

**The apophatic tradition**. The apophatic tradition (Pseudo-Dionysius, Meister Eckhart, Nicholas of Cusa, John of the Cross, others) approaches the divine by negation rather than affirmation — God is *not* finite, *not* limited, *not* contained. It parallels Infinity's cosmic-impersonal content within Christian framing.

**Nicholas of Cusa's "learned ignorance"**. **Nicholas of Cusa**'s *De Docta Ignorantia* (1440) treated the divine infinite and the coincidence of opposites in the infinite divine, paralleling Infinity's cosmic-impersonal content.

**Pseudo-Dionysius's apophatic theology**. The fifth–sixth-century Pseudo-Dionysian corpus gave the foundational articulation of Christian apophatic theology, influential across the later tradition.

The apophatic tradition parallels Infinity within Christian framing, and is itself structurally distinct from the cataphatic supernatural-deity framework that Infinity rejects.

### Islamic la-mahdud (the unbounded)

The Islamic theological-philosophical tradition develops cosmic infinity through the **la-mahdud** (the unbounded, the infinite) framework.

**The Islamic infinite divine**. The tradition treats Allah as la-mahdud (unbounded), with the divine attributes of omniscience, omnipotence, and eternality.

**The Sufi mystical tradition**. The Sufi tradition (Ibn Arabi, Rumi, others) developed cosmic infinity through *wahdat al-wujud* (unity of being); Ibn Arabi's framework parallels Infinity's cosmic-impersonal content within Islamic framing.

### Greek apeiron tradition

The Greek philosophical tradition develops cosmic infinity through the **apeiron** (the boundless, the infinite) tradition.

**Anaximander's apeiron**. **Anaximander** (c. 610–546 BCE) proposed the *apeiron* as the foundational principle of cosmic existence — an indefinite, unlimited substrate from which all finite things emerge and to which they return — paralleling Infinity's infinite-cosmological content.

**The Atomist infinite worlds**. The Greek Atomists (Leucippus, Democritus, Epicurus, Lucretius) held infinite cosmic extent and infinite numbers of cosmic worlds, paralleling Infinity's spatial-infinity content.

**Aristotle's potential vs. actual infinity**. **Aristotle**'s view that only potential infinity is legitimate is treated above under *Modern reinterpretations* — one Greek-philosophical articulation of infinity.

**Plotinus's One**. **Plotinus** (c. 204–270 CE) developed the Neoplatonic One as the infinite-unitary cosmic foundational reality, paralleling Infinity's cosmic-impersonal content.

### Chinese Dao infinity

The Chinese philosophical-religious tradition develops cosmic infinity through the **Dao** framework.

**The Dao as cosmic-impersonal-foundational-reality**. The Daoist tradition (Laozi, Zhuangzi, and later Daoism) treats the Dao as the cosmic-impersonal foundational reality that pervades and constitutes all existence — *wuming* (nameless), *wuxing* (formless), *wuwei* (non-acting), beyond all predicates and descriptions — paralleling Infinity's cosmic-impersonal content.

**The cosmic-cyclic content**. Chinese cosmic-cyclic frameworks (yin-yang alternation, the five-phase cycle) parallel Infinity's temporal-infinite content.

### Indigenous cosmic-infinite traditions

Several indigenous traditions preserve cosmic-infinite content within their own framings.

**Aboriginal Australian traditions** preserve cosmic infinity through the Dreamtime framework. The detailed treatment lives in the [Mass Effect](../mass-effect/) entry's *Comparative observations*.

**Mesoamerican traditions** preserve it through cosmic-cyclic frameworks (the Maya Long Count, the Aztec cosmic cycles, others). The detailed treatment lives in the [Cosmic Chain](../cosmic-chain/) and [Mass Effect](../mass-effect/) entries.

**North American indigenous traditions** preserve it within their own framings.

**African traditions** preserve it through their own cosmological frameworks.

Each indigenous tradition reflects the cosmological situation in its own cultural-religious framing.

### The convergence

The corpus reads the global recurrence of cosmic-infinity frameworks across cultural-religious traditions as evidence of a broader pattern. The mainstream explanation — that each tradition developed its content through internal religious-philosophical processes plus cultural diffusion — is, on the corpus's reading, insufficient to account for the breadth and the structural parallels: the cosmic-impersonal foundational reality, the rejection of anthropomorphic deity in the most philosophically developed traditions, and the systematic relationship between infinity and cosmic structure, recurring across geographically and chronologically separated traditions.

On the framework's reading, the pattern preserves a common memory of the cosmological situation Infinity articulates, each tradition holding it in its own terms — the Hindu Brahman/Atman framework most directly among them.

The source itself explicitly identifies several of these traditions with the framework: *"Those who think of 'God' as Infinity, as most oriental religions teach, are right, in as much as it represents a concept without identity, and without consciousness of our own existence, or any other for that matter."* The reference to "most oriental religions" reads the Hindu, Buddhist, Daoist, and wider Asian traditions as preserving accurate cosmological content within their own framings.

The corpus does not reject the whole mainstream account. Cultural diffusion did occur, and the traditions show both common structural features (preserving the original framework) and culturally specific elaborations (reflecting diffusion and local development). What the framework adds is the underlying cosmological situation behind the commonalities — which the mainstream, lacking that framework, has had to attribute to independent development plus diffusion.
