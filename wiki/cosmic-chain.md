The **Cosmic Chain** is the framework concept the Wheel of Heaven corpus uses to describe the cosmological situation in which the Elohim civilization operates: an indefinitely extended sequence of created-and-creating civilizations across cosmic time. Each civilization that reaches scientific maturity eventually creates new humanities on suitable worlds, which in turn become creator civilizations themselves, continuing the chain. During the Aries age (c. 2,160–1,200 BCE on the corpus's reckoning) the Elohim discovered that they had themselves been created by a prior civilization rather than being the originators of the biological-creation pattern they were extending across the galaxy — they were the current local instance of a recurring process of unknown but very long duration. The chain extends indefinitely backward through prior creators and forward through future creations, with no findable beginning and no findable end.

The framework integrates several strands of source material. The Raëlian source provides the principal warrant in a direct statement: *"The Elohim were created by people from another planet, who had been created by other people coming from another planet, and so on to Infinity."* It addresses and rejects the conventional theological question — "who created the first creator?" — treating it as grammatically well-formed but resting on a presupposition (that there is a first creator) that does not match reality's structure. The Lord's Prayer's central petition — *"thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven"* (Matthew 6:10) — is read as a compressed statement of the chain's cyclical structure: "heaven" is the home world that has already become a creator civilization, "earth" is Earth's projected trajectory toward the same threshold.

The Cosmic Chain is the broadest cosmological framework in the corpus's apparatus. It is wider than the Cosmic Competition (the evaluation phase in which a creator civilization assesses its created humanities for inheritance qualification), the precessional-ages framework (Earth's chronological position within the chain), and the Earth project (the alliance's work on Earth) — each of these operates within it as one aspect of a larger pattern. Its distinctive features are its infinity in both temporal directions, its cyclical-recursive structure (each cycle repeats the same shape of created-developing-creating), its theological implications (the rejection of the "first creator" question, the reframing of "God," the position on humanity's cosmic role), and its integration with the wider Wheel of Heaven framework — the Cosmic Competition, the Doubled Signature, the Aquarian-age developments, and the Embassy all operating within it.

The reading is interpretive construction.{{ footnote(id="1") }} The Raëlian source provides the basic premise directly{{ cite(id="extraterrestrials-took-me-to-their-planet", text="[1]", title="Vorilhon (Raël), *Extra-Terrestrials Took Me to Their Planet* (1975), 'Neither God nor Soul' — 'and so on to Infinity'") }} but does not develop the systematic framework the corpus articulates — the integration with the Cosmic Competition, the Lord's Prayer reading, the engagement with the chain's epistemological and theological implications, and the projection of Earth's future trajectory. On the wider questions the framework touches, mainstream thought has its own long development: cosmic infinity from Bruno's *De l'infinito universo et mondi* (1584){{ cite(id="bruno-infinite-universe", text="[2]", title="Bruno, *On the Infinite Universe and Worlds* (1584)") }} through contemporary multiverse cosmology; theological engagement with creation and divine eternality, against which the chain's "no first creator" position diverges from Western traditions but aligns with Eastern beginningless-cycle frameworks; and the panspermia tradition, in which Crick and Orgel's directed-panspermia hypothesis (1973){{ cite(id="crick-orgel-directed-panspermia", text="[3]", title="Crick & Orgel, 'Directed Panspermia' (Icarus, 1973)") }} overlaps with the chain's directed-creation premise. The corpus's reading is distinctive in its recursive-cyclical articulation and its integration with the Wheel of Heaven framework's operational history.

## Etymology and naming

The Cosmic Chain has no single source-material designation; the corpus condenses several related phrasings under the standard term.

### "Cosmic Chain" as the principal designation

**"Cosmic Chain"** is the corpus's principal term. "Cosmic" marks the scale — across cosmic time, multiple civilizations, multiple worlds; "Chain" marks the linked-sequential character, each civilization a link connected to prior and subsequent ones. The term is corpus-internal and does not appear in the Raëlian source under this designation.

### Source-material terminology

The Raëlian source uses several terms for what the corpus organizes as the Cosmic Chain:

- **"Recurring cosmic process"** — the broad cyclical pattern
- **"Cycle of creators"** — the pattern of created civilizations becoming creators
- **"On to Infinity"** — the infinite-recursion character of the chain
- **"As above, so below"** — the Hermetic phrase the source occasionally invokes (without its Hermetic theological content)
- **"On Earth as it is in heaven"** — the Lord's Prayer phrase read as a compressed statement of the chain

### Related conceptual terminology

Several related terms operate within the framework:

- **"Chain of creators"** — the backward-extending sequence of prior creator civilizations
- **"Chain of created"** — the forward-extending sequence of future creators (currently civilizations like Earth's humanity, in their pre-maturity phase)
- **"Cosmic creative cycle"** — the recurring pattern that produces new creator civilizations
- **"Inheritance threshold"** — the point at which a created civilization becomes capable of becoming a creator (the Cosmic Competition's evaluation criterion)
- **"Generational cosmology"** — the framework the chain produces, in which civilizations stand in genealogical relationships across cosmic time

## Conventional understanding

The Cosmic Chain is largely a corpus-internal category; its points of contact with conventional scholarship are the questions of cosmic infinity and the origin of life on Earth.

### Mainstream Western theological tradition

Mainstream Western theology positions a creator deity at the origin of the cosmic process.

**Christian tradition** posits a single uncreated creator (God) originating the cosmos through *creatio ex nihilo*; the classical formulations (Augustine, Aquinas, the medieval scholastics) develop divine eternality and the priority of creator over creation. The chain's "no first creator" position diverges sharply from it.

**Jewish tradition** likewise posits a creator deity (YHWH/Elohim), with Genesis 1 as the foundational text and elaboration in Maimonides, Crescas, and the rabbinic philosophical tradition. Kabbalah adds the *Ein Sof* (the infinite divine source) and the *Sefirot* (emanated divine attributes), which overlap structurally with the chain's infinite-cosmology framing though the theological content differs.

**Islamic tradition** posits Allah as the single uncreated creator, on the Qur'anic creation material, with elaboration in the Mu'tazilite, Ash'arite, Maturidite, and Sufi traditions. Again the chain's position diverges.

The framework's relationship to Western theology is one of divergence on the "first creator" question while overlapping with Eastern cyclic-cosmology traditions (treated under *Comparative observations*).

### Mainstream cosmology

Mainstream cosmology addresses cosmic origin, infinity, and structure.

**The Big Bang model** posits the universe's origin in a hot, dense state about 13.8 billion years ago, confirmed through galactic redshift, the cosmic microwave background, light-element abundances, and large-scale structure. **Cosmic inflation** (Guth, Linde, Steinhardt and others, from about 1980) extends it, predicting cosmic flatness and isotropy. **Eternal inflation and multiverse cosmology** (Linde, Vilenkin) proposes that our observable universe is one region within a larger multiverse — overlapping with the chain's recursive structure though differing in content. **Cyclic cosmologies** — the Steinhardt-Turok ekpyrotic model,{{ cite(id="steinhardt-turok-endless-universe", text="[14]", title="Steinhardt & Turok, *Endless Universe* (2007) — the ekpyrotic cyclic model") }} Penrose's conformal cyclic cosmology,{{ cite(id="penrose-cycles-of-time", text="[15]", title="Penrose, *Cycles of Time* (2011) — conformal cyclic cosmology") }} and others — propose multiple successive Big Bang events rather than a single origin, with the underlying spacetime-structure questions treated in Hawking and Penrose's exchange.{{ cite(id="hawking-penrose-nature-space-time", text="[16]", title="Hawking & Penrose, *The Nature of Space and Time* (1996)") }} These overlap with the chain's recursive structure at the cosmological level. The framework does not contradict observational cosmology; it extends beyond it into the chain's civilizational-recursive content.

### The infinite-universe philosophical tradition

The philosophical tradition has long treated cosmic infinity. **Giordano Bruno**, in *De l'infinito universo et mondi* (1584), argued for an infinite universe of infinite inhabited worlds; his cosmological-theological doctrines contributed to his execution by the Roman Inquisition in 1600. **Immanuel Kant**, in the *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781), placed cosmic infinity among the antinomies of pure reason. The framework aligns with the tradition's recognition of infinity as a real cosmological feature while extending beyond it through the chain's civilizational-recursive content.

### Contemporary panspermia research

**Panspermia** — that life on Earth came from cosmic sources rather than autochthonous emergence — has developed over the past century. **Classical panspermia** (Arrhenius, early 20th century){{ cite(id="arrhenius-worlds-in-the-making", text="[4]", title="Arrhenius, *Worlds in the Making* (1908) — classical panspermia") }} proposed that microbial life travels across cosmic distances on dust or meteoric material; Hoyle and Wickramasinghe extended this into cometary panspermia.{{ cite(id="hoyle-wickramasinghe-lifecloud", text="[5]", title="Hoyle & Wickramasinghe, *Lifecloud* (1978)") }} **Directed panspermia** (Crick and Orgel, "Directed Panspermia," *Icarus* 19 (1973): 341–346) proposed that Earth's life was deliberately seeded by an extraterrestrial civilization rather than arriving naturally. Developed by Francis Crick (Nobel-laureate co-discoverer of DNA structure) and Leslie Orgel (a principal scientist of early-Earth chemical biology), it addressed the universality of the genetic code, the dependence of all Earth life on molybdenum (of limited terrestrial abundance), and biochemical features that appear well-suited to deliberate engineering. The chain aligns with directed panspermia — both read Earth's life as deliberately produced — and extends it through the recursive content: the Elohim themselves created by a prior civilization, backward indefinitely.

### Mainstream Western philosophical engagement with cyclic cosmology

Western philosophy has engaged cyclic cosmology less than the Eastern traditions. **The Stoic eternal-recurrence doctrine** (Chrysippus and others) held that the cosmos undergoes successive cycles of conflagration and renewal, each reproducing the last in identical form — the principal Western antecedent for cyclic cosmology.{{ cite(id="long-sedley-hellenistic-philosophers", text="[7]", title="Long & Sedley, *The Hellenistic Philosophers* (1987) — the Stoic eternal-recurrence doctrine") }} **Nietzsche**, in *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* (1883–1885) and *The Will to Power* (posthumous), treated eternal return as both cosmological hypothesis and existential test{{ cite(id="nietzsche-zarathustra", text="[8]", title="Nietzsche, *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* (1883–1885) — the eternal-recurrence doctrine") }} — a modern Western engagement that differs from the chain's civilizational-recursive content. Contemporary cyclic cosmologies (Steinhardt-Turok, Penrose) are the principal current scientific engagement. The framework aligns structurally with the cyclic principle while developing the civilizational-recursive content as its own contribution.

## In primary sources

The framework's primary-source material is a set of Raëlian passages on the chain of creators, the rejection of the "first creator" question, and the cyclical-recursive structure.

### The fundamental source-material statement

The Raëlian source's principal statement appears in *Extra-Terrestrials Took Me to Their Planet* (1975), in "Neither God nor Soul":

> *"The Elohim were created by people from another planet, who had been created by other people coming from another planet, and so on to Infinity."*

This single statement is the framework's principal warrant.{{ cite(id="the-book-which-tells-the-truth", text="[9]", title="Vorilhon (Raël), *The Book Which Tells the Truth* (1974) — the Aries-discovery and cosmological framing") }} The corpus develops it into the full Cosmic Chain framework. Its features:

- The Elohim are identified as created, not as autonomous originators of the pattern
- Their prior creators were themselves created, not uncreated first creators
- The chain extends "to Infinity," not terminating at a prior point
- Each step is structurally identical: a civilization is created, then itself becomes a creator

### The "no first creator" position

The source rejects the conventional question — "who created the first creator?" — as grammatically well-formed but resting on a presupposition that does not match reality. Western theology presupposes a first, uncreated creator (the *causa prima* of the scholastics); the chain rejects this. It extends backward indefinitely just as it extends forward indefinitely — no ultimate origin, no ultimate end. The question is, on this view, like asking "what is north of the North Pole?" The position is distinctive within Western philosophy and theology while overlapping with Eastern beginningless-cycle frameworks.{{ footnote(id="2") }}

### The Lord's Prayer reading

The Lord's Prayer's central petition — *"Thy kingdom come; thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven"* (Matthew 6:10, with parallel in Luke 11:2) — is read as a compressed statement of the chain's structure. The source's reading: *"In 'heaven', on the creators' planet, the scientists eventually became the ruling group and then created other intelligent beings. The same thing will happen on Earth. The torch will be taken up again. This prayer, which has been repeated time and time again without anyone understanding its profound meaning, now takes on its full significance: On Earth, as it is in heaven."*

"Heaven" is the Elohim home world, a civilization that has already passed through the stages Earth is traversing; "earth" is Earth, now in a stage the home world has passed. The petition is for Earth to reach the home world's level — the point at which its humanity can itself create new intelligent life. The reading is shared with the Cosmic Competition entry but operates within the wider chain: the Competition is the evaluation phase, the chain the cyclical structure within which it sits.

### The "as above, so below" Hermetic reading

The source occasionally invokes the Hermetic phrase **"as above, so below"** (the *Tabula Smaragdina*, or Emerald Tablet) in connection with the framework. The phrase belongs to the Western Hermetic tradition from late antiquity, with later Renaissance development (Ficino, Pico della Mirandola, Bruno, and others).{{ cite(id="yates-giordano-bruno-hermetic", text="[17]", title="Yates, *Giordano Bruno and the Hermetic Tradition* (1964)") }} The framework keeps the recursive insight the phrase preserves — what occurs at one level of the creative hierarchy repeats at the level it produces — without adopting the wider Hermetic theological apparatus.

### The Aries-discovery passage

The source describes the policy shift produced by the Aries-age discovery:

> *"Because of recent discoveries, the creators decided to appear as little as possible in order not to influence the destiny of Man too much, so that they could see if they would reach the age of scientific knowledge on their own. So, the creators began to use increasingly discreet means of communicating with humans, as in the method of feeding Elijah using 'homing' ravens. This was the beginning of a gigantic experiment throughout the galaxy in which several humanities are in competition. The creators decided to appear less often, while at the same time reinforcing the authority and reputation of their ambassadors - the prophets - by using miracles."*

The passage establishes several things at once: the discovery is real and specific, not metaphorical; it produced a deliberate shift in operational practice; the shift inaugurated the cosmic-competition framework; the framework operates "throughout the galaxy," not only on Earth; and it applies to all the parallel humanities the alliance had created. The Aries-discovery is the operational origin of the chain in the corpus's narrative — before it, the Elohim assumed they originated the creation pattern; after it, they recognized themselves as participants in a much wider one.

### The infinity-of-cosmos passages

The source provides material on the infinite cosmos within which the chain operates:

- **Infinite in space** — no center and no boundary in any direction
- **Infinite in time** — no beginning and no end
- **Infinite hierarchy of scales** — intelligent civilizations at every scale, from infinitely small to infinitely large
- **Infinity as the foundational feature** — the local features, the chain among them, operate within the infinite structure

This material is treated more fully in the wider Wheel of Heaven cosmology; the chain entry's contribution is registering that the chain operates within this infinite structure rather than being a complete account of the cosmos.

### The cyclical-cosmic-future passages

The source also provides material on humanity's future role:

- **Earth's projected transition** from created to creator civilization, following the Aquarian-age inheritance event
- **Future creation by humanity** of new humanities on suitable worlds
- **The pattern's continuation** as those future civilizations become creators in turn
- **Infinite forward extension** of the chain through these cycles

This is the framework's warrant for the chain's forward extension; the corpus develops it through integration with the Cosmic Competition, the Aquarian-age developments, and the projected post-inheritance trajectory.

## The concept's content

The framework integrates the source strands into a coherent account.

### The basic cyclical-recursive structure

**The fundamental cycle.** Each civilization follows a structurally identical trajectory:

1. **Creation** — created by a prior creator civilization through operational interventions (as in the Elohim's work on Earth)
2. **Development** — developing across cosmic time, through ages analogous to Earth's precessional ages
3. **Discovery** — discovering, at some point, that it has itself been created (as in the Elohim's Aries-age discovery)
4. **Maturation** — reaching the threshold of scientific maturity required to become a creator
5. **Inheritance** — receiving the accumulated knowledge of its creators (as in Earth's projected Aquarian-age inheritance)
6. **Creator-civilization phase** — conducting its own creation projects on suitable worlds
7. **Continuation** — its created humanities developing through the same trajectory, indefinitely

**Structural identity across cycles.** Every instance has the same shape — creation, development, discovery, maturation, inheritance, creator phase, continuation — even where specific contents differ. The cycle is what repeats, not the contents.

**Infinite extension in both directions.** The chain extends indefinitely backward through prior creators and forward through future creations; neither direction reaches a findable limit. The cosmos has no first creator and no final creator.

### The infinite-recursion character

**The "no first creator" position.** The chain extends backward without findable beginning; the question "who created the first creator?" is rejected as resting on a presupposition that does not match reality — distinct from Western theology, aligned with Eastern cyclic cosmologies.

**The infinite forward extension.** Symmetrically, each matured civilization becomes a creator and produces civilizations that will do the same, without findable end.

**The implication for Earth.** Earth's humanity was created by the Elohim, who were created by their predecessors, and so on backward; Earth will itself become a creator, whose creations will become creators, and so on forward. Earth's situation is one link in a pattern operating across the cosmos for an unknown but very long span in each direction.

### The integration with Aries-discovery

The chain's operational origin is the Aries-age discovery (c. 2,160–1,200 BCE), when the Elohim recognized that they had themselves been created.{{ footnote(id="3") }} The source does not specify what evidence established this — only the consequence: the Elohim's self-understanding shifted, changing their policy toward the humanities they had created.

The discovery produced several operational changes:

- **The shift from direct to indirect contact** — a humanity carried to scientific maturity by sustained intervention has not demonstrated it; the pattern presumably requires each humanity to mature with enough autonomy from its creators to count as genuine.
- **The prophetic tradition** — the principal mechanism for indirect contact, maintaining communication while preserving the appearance of autonomy the pattern required.
- **The cultivation of multiple lineages** — Hebrew, Persian, Greek, Indian, Chinese, Mesoamerican — reflecting the cosmic-competition evaluation function.

## Modern reinterpretations

The framework's modern reinterpretive landscape has several strands.

### Mainstream cosmology infinite-universe questions

Bruno's *De l'infinito universo et mondi* (1584) argued for an infinite universe of infinite inhabited worlds and cost him his life in 1600; he is the principal early-modern antecedent of the contemporary discussion. Kant, in the *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781), placed the finite-or-infinite question among the antinomies, arguing it cannot be settled by pure reason alone since both answers generate contradictions.{{ cite(id="kant-critique-pure-reason", text="[10]", title="Kant, *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781) — the antinomies on cosmic infinity") }} Contemporary multiverse cosmology (Linde, Vilenkin, Tegmark) develops the question scientifically — through eternal-inflation models (our universe one region of a larger multiverse), string-theory-landscape models (physical constants varying across regions), and many-worlds quantum mechanics. Tegmark's mathematical-universe hypothesis (*Our Mathematical Universe*, 2014) holds that all mathematically consistent universes are physically real.{{ cite(id="tegmark-mathematical-universe", text="[11]", title="Tegmark, *Our Mathematical Universe* (2014)") }}{{ cite(id="vilenkin-many-worlds-in-one", text="[12]", title="Vilenkin, *Many Worlds in One* (2006)") }}{{ cite(id="linde-inflation-quantum-cosmology", text="[13]", title="Linde, *Inflation and Quantum Cosmology* (1990)") }} The chain does not contradict observational cosmology while extending beyond it into civilizational-recursive content.

### The steady-state vs. Big Bang debate

The steady-state model (Hoyle, Bondi, Gold, 1940s–50s) held that the universe has no origin, new matter being continuously created to keep density constant — a serious competitor to the Big Bang through the mid-20th century. The Big Bang model (Lemaître, Gamow, and others) placed the origin in a hot, dense state about 13.8 billion years ago. Penzias and Wilson's 1965 detection of the cosmic microwave background settled the question in the Big Bang's favor. Cyclic alternatives have continued since: the Steinhardt-Turok ekpyrotic model makes the Big Bang one event in an ongoing cycle, and Penrose's conformal cyclic cosmology proposes another cyclic framework. The chain is structurally compatible with these cyclic models, though its content is civilizational rather than purely physical.

### Contemporary panspermia and directed-panspermia hypotheses

Classical panspermia (Arrhenius, *Worlds in the Making*, 1908) proposed cosmic transmission of microbial life; Crick and Orgel's "Directed Panspermia" (*Icarus* 19, 1973: 341–346) proposed deliberate seeding by an extraterrestrial civilization, citing the universality of the genetic code, the molybdenum dependence of Earth life, and biochemical features suggestive of engineering. Contemporary research continues on microbial survivability in space, transmission vectors, and whether Earth's life is unique or part of a wider pattern. The chain aligns with directed panspermia on the deliberate-creation premise and extends it through the recursive content — the Elohim themselves created by a prior civilization, backward indefinitely.

### Sendy on cosmic recursion

**Jean Sendy** read the Hebrew Bible's references to multiple cosmic powers as preserving fragmentary memory of the wider cosmic context, and treated the Hebrew cosmological framework as compatible with cosmic-recursive content, integrated with the alliance-mediated history he reconstructed. His development of the chain is more limited than its contemporary articulation, though his method is compatible with it.

### Biglino on cosmic recursion

**Mauro Biglino**'s strict-translational reading of the Hebrew Bible's plural cosmic powers is compatible with the chain but does not develop the recursive-cyclical content explicitly.

### Wallis on cosmic recursion

**Paul Anthony Wallis**'s work on the alliance-mediated history touches cosmic-recursive questions, mainly through ancient texts' references to cosmic-political pluralism; his development of the chain is more limited than the contemporary articulation, though compatible with it.

### The broader ancient-astronaut tradition

The ancient-astronaut tradition has engaged cosmic-recursive questions variously: von Däniken (*Chariots of the Gods*, 1968) treats the cosmic-political situation as involving advanced extraterrestrial civilizations; Sitchin (*The 12th Planet*, 1976) develops a different Anunnaki-Nibiru cosmology with little specific engagement with the chain; other contemporary writers add their own treatments. The corpus's framework is distinctive within this tradition through its recursive-cyclical content and its integration with the Wheel of Heaven operational history.

In sum, the framework is empirically consistent with mainstream cosmology even where its claims exceed what has been confirmed; aligned with directed panspermia on deliberate creation while extending beyond it; compatible with contemporary cyclic cosmologies (Steinhardt-Turok, Penrose) while differing on civilizational content; distinct from Western theology on the "first creator" question; aligned with Eastern cyclic-cosmology traditions (below); and distinctive within the ancient-astronaut tradition through its recursive-cyclical content.

## Comparative observations

Cyclical cosmologies appear across many traditions, with structural parallels to the chain. The corpus reads this recurrence as fragmentary preservation of a genuine underlying reality.

### Hindu cyclic cosmology: kalpa-yuga

The Hindu tradition preserves the most developed cyclic cosmology in the world's religions. The four **yugas** — Krita (Satya), Treta, Dvapara, Kali — form the cosmic developmental cycle, of 1,728,000; 1,296,000; 864,000; and 432,000 years respectively, together comprising a **Mahayuga** of 4,320,000 years. One thousand mahayugas form a **Kalpa** of roughly 4.32 billion years — one "day of Brahma," followed by an equally long "night" of cosmic dissolution. The cosmos undergoes infinite successive Kalpas of creation, development, dissolution, and re-creation — structurally similar to the chain's recursive content. Various texts describe multiple Brahma-systems, each Brahma within its own cosmic system, paralleling the chain's multiple creator civilizations. The framework reads the Hindu cosmology as preserving fragmentary memory of the recursive structure in Hindu terms,{{ cite(id="bhagavad-gita", text="[18]", title="*The Bhagavad Gita* (trans. Easwaran) — the cosmic-cycle framework") }}{{ cite(id="doniger-hindu-myths", text="[19]", title="Doniger, *Hindu Myths: A Sourcebook* (1975)") }} the yuga durations and kalpa cosmology being the tradition's distinctive elaboration of the underlying pattern.

### Buddhist beginningless cycles

The Buddhist tradition treats *saṃsāra* as **beginningless** (*anavarāgra*) — not lacking a beginning in absolute time but having no findable first moment; the Pali *anamatagga* ("without findable beginning") captures it. Buddhist cosmology develops elaborate *kalpa* cycles (paralleling the Hindu kalpa) and treats Śākyamuni as the most recent of a long series of buddhas across cosmic time, with Maitreya the next — a succession that parallels the chain's recursive structure. The Mahāyāna preserves extensive multiple-worlds material in its buddha-fields (treated more fully in the [Cosmic Competition](../cosmic-competition/) entry). The framework reads the beginningless-cycle tradition as parallel content,{{ cite(id="williams-mahayana-buddhism", text="[20]", title="Williams, *Mahāyāna Buddhism* (2008) — the beginningless saṃsāra and buddha-field cosmology") }}{{ cite(id="buswell-lopez-princeton-buddhism", text="[21]", title="Buswell & Lopez, *The Princeton Dictionary of Buddhism* (2014)") }} the "without findable beginning" framing being structurally identical with the chain's "no first creator" position.

### Jain anadi cosmology

The Jain tradition preserves an **anadi** ("without beginning") cosmology: the universe is eternal and uncreated, with no origin and no ending, undergoing cyclic phases without ultimate beginning or termination. Its cosmic time-cycle (the *kalachakra*, "wheel of time") comprises alternating ascending and descending half-cycles of six ages each, continuing indefinitely, and the cosmos contains infinite souls (*jīva*), each on its own trajectory across the cycles. The *anadi* framework parallels the chain's "no first creator" position closely — both reject the presupposition of an ultimate cosmic origin and build alternative content within an eternal-universe frame.{{ cite(id="dundas-the-jains", text="[22]", title="Dundas, *The Jains* (2002) — the anadi beginningless-and-endless time-cycle cosmology") }}

### Stoic eternal recurrence

The Stoic tradition (Chrysippus and others) held that the cosmos undergoes successive cycles of conflagration (*ekpyrosis*) and renewal (*palingenesis*), each reproducing the last in identical form — the same events in the same sequence, the same individuals living the same lives. The *ekpyrosis* consumes the whole cosmos before renewal begins. The chain differs in treating cycles as structurally identical but with different contents, while the recursive-cyclical pattern is aligned.

### Hermetic "as above, so below"

The Hermetic tradition preserves cosmic correspondence in **"as above, so below"**: the macrocosm corresponds to the microcosm, the celestial to the terrestrial, the divine to the human. The phrase's principal source is the *Tabula Smaragdina* (Emerald Tablet), attributed to Hermes Trismegistus and preserved in medieval Latin and Arabic versions; it was elaborated across the Renaissance Hermetic tradition (Ficino, Pico della Mirandola, Bruno, Dee, and others). The correspondence principle parallels the chain's recursive content — what occurs at one level of the creative hierarchy repeats at the level it produces — which the framework keeps without the wider Hermetic apparatus. The Lord's Prayer's "thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven" preserves the same insight in Christian form; the framework reads both as fragmentary memory of the same recursive structure.

### Mesoamerican cyclic cosmologies

The Aztec tradition preserves the doctrine of five successive cosmic ages (the **five suns**), each ending in catastrophe — preserved principally in the *Codex Chimalpopoca* (whose *Leyenda de los Soles* gives the fullest account). They are the Jaguar Sun (ended by jaguar attacks), the Wind Sun (hurricanes), the Rain Sun (fiery rain), the Water Sun (flood), and the present Movement Sun (projected to end in earthquakes). The Maya Long Count preserves cyclic-temporal content — the 360-day *tun*, the 7,200-day *katun*, the 144,000-day *baktun*, and longer cycles — integrated with cosmological-religious material. Other Mesoamerican traditions preserve further cyclic material, treated in the [Antediluvian](../antediluvian/) and [Great Flood](../great-flood/) entries' comparative observations. The framework reads these as parallel content.{{ cite(id="carrasco-religions-mesoamerica", text="[23]", title="Carrasco, *Religions of Mesoamerica* — the five-suns successive-world cosmology") }}{{ cite(id="tedlock-popol-vuh", text="[24]", title="Tedlock (trans.), *Popol Vuh* (1996)") }}

### Norse cyclic cosmology

The Norse tradition preserves cyclic material in the **Ragnarök-and-renewal** framework. Ragnarök is the cosmic catastrophe in which the principal Aesir die in battle with their adversaries — Loki, the Fenrir wolf, the Midgard serpent, the giants — preserved principally in the *Poetic Edda*'s *Völuspá* and Snorri Sturluson's *Prose Edda* (*Gylfaginning*).{{ cite(id="poetic-edda", text="[25]", title="*The Poetic Edda* (trans. Larrington) — Völuspá on Ragnarök and renewal") }} After Ragnarök a new earth rises from the sea, repopulated by surviving deities (Vidar, Vali, Modi, Magni) and surviving humans (Líf and Lífþrasir, who sheltered in Hoddmímis holt). Various readings treat the cosmology as cyclic rather than linear-eschatological.{{ cite(id="lindow-norse-mythology", text="[26]", title="Lindow, *Norse Mythology* (2001)") }} The framework reads it as parallel content in a distinctive cultural form.

### The convergence

The corpus's working position is that the global recurrence of cyclic cosmologies across traditions is meaningful — evidence of a genuine underlying reality.{{ footnote(id="6") }}{{ cite(id="hannah-greek-roman-calendars", text="[27]", title="Hannah, *Greek and Roman Calendars* (2005) — classical cyclic-time and the Great Year") }} The mainstream explanation — internal religious-philosophical development plus cultural diffusion — is read as insufficient to account for the breadth and the structural parallels (the recursive-cyclical pattern, the eternal-recurrence implications, the multiple-cosmic-cycles content) that recur across geographically and chronologically separated traditions.

Each tradition, on the framework's reading, preserves the same memory in its own terms: the Hindu and Buddhist traditions most directly relative to the recursive content; the Jain *anadi* tradition the "no first creator" position; the Stoic tradition the eternal-recurrence content within Western philosophy; the Hermetic tradition the correspondence principle; the Mesoamerican and Norse traditions cyclic material within their own channels.

The corpus does not reject the whole mainstream account. Cultural diffusion certainly occurred, and the cosmologies show both common structural features and culturally specific elaborations. What the framework adds is the underlying historical-cosmic situation that produced the commonalities — a situation the mainstream account, lacking the corpus's cosmological framework, has had to attribute to independent development and diffusion.
